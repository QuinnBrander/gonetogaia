Overview
Welcome to the world of Gone to Gaia, an epic campaign-
style board game that you can play cooperatively as 2
players, or experience on your own in a dedicated solo
mode. In this game you will take on the role of delvers,
trained explorers, that can navigate the perilous world
under the mist known as the Tide.
Each game session will alternate between going on a quest
and returning to town. When on a quest you will explore
the Tide and solve combat-focused puzzles in order to
complete your goals. Whether or not your delvers survive
the quest, the story will return you to the town of Birds Nest,
where you will get to know its citizens and help solve the
unfolding mysteries that threaten their existence.
Your overall goal is to complete a variety of quests that will
help progress the story to its conclusion.

Equipment Tray Organization
Each Delver has a set of 70 Equipment Cards, divided
among 14 different Equipment types. Each Equipment type
forms its own 5-card deck. Store these 14 decks in one of
the two provided card trays, which contains 14 slots. At
the start of each Quest, arrange every Equipment deck in
ascending rank order from 1 to 5. During a Quest, players
will often gain upgraded cards from these decks, placing
previous upgrades at the back of the appropriate deck. Be
sure to reset the Equipment trays before the start of the
next Quest whenever this occurs.
Note: During the campaign, players may be instructed to
replace an Equipment deck with an upgraded version.
When this happens, return the old Equipment deck of that
type to the box, then place the new cards in ascending rank
order from 1 to 5 in the matching slot of the tray. These new
cards remain there for the rest of the campaign.

The Rising Tide Book
The Rising Tide Book holds a majority of the game’s story
and instructions on how to proceed through each quest.
Throughout the game the Quest Card will guide you to a
specific page of the Rising Tide Booklet. The start of each
quest in the booklet will start with the Quest Introduction.
Waves
Each quest will take you into the Tide. A Quest is broken
up into Waves that you will be guided through in the Tide
Booklet. The Tide Booklet will give you a quick overview of
the wave, your wave goal, any wave setup instructions, and
what to do when you end a wave.

Wave Intro
At the start of each Wave, players may encounter a short
story entry that provides the setting or context for that
wave. Players may also be instructed to resolve a specific
Scenario Card. To do so, find the matching card in the
Scenario Deck, read it aloud, and discuss the available
options. After choosing an option, flip the card over, read
and resolve the matching resolution, then discard the card.
Wave Goal
Each Quest will start on Wave 1. To move onto the following
waves, you must complete the Wave Goal. Each wave goal
can vary. Most Wave Goals will require you just to survive,
however some may require you to defeat specific creatures,
pick up specific items, or complete something that is
relevant to the story. Once you have completed the Wave
Goal, you can move onto the End of Wave instructions.

Wave Setup
Each Wave will require you to setup the Tide board. To do
this you will follow the instructions of the The Wave Setup.
Often this will require you to search for creatures in the
Creature deck and spawn them. Before spawning creatures,
make sure to follow any additional instructions listed here.
Searching the Creature Deck
When searching the Creature Deck for the creatures listed
in the Wave Setup, make sure to search for the cards that
have a matching name. Sometimes there will be fewer
cards required for setup than what you find in the Creature
Deck. If this happens, simply randomly select the number
that you need and place them to the side to prepare to be
spawned. The remaining cards can stay in the creature deck.
Once all necessary creature cards are selected and placed
to the side, you are now ready to spawn creatures.
Spawning Creatures
Once you have selected the listed creature cards from the
Creature Deck, it’s time to place them on the tide board.
Often the Wave Setup will instruct you to draw a spawn card
to determine how they spawn. However sometimes, like in
your first encounter, it will simply have a setup for you in
the book. Whether it be through a spawn card or the book,
understanding how to spawn creatures will be the same.

Fighting Waves
1. Hero phase
Each Wave starts with the Hero Phase. The Hero Phase
allows players to take turns. A player’s turn is broken up
into 2 steps, Movement and Action, that they can execute
with their chosen Delver. These steps can be done in any
order, but once a player has completed both steps their turn
is over, and the next player’s turn begins.
A Delver’s Tools
To understand how to execute your turn, you must first have
an understanding of the tools a delver has at their disposal.
When controlling their Delver players can use their suit
and hero abilities, equipment cards, element tokens, and
consumable tokens to help them reach their goals.
Suit & Hero Abilities
When you start your first game you will choose a Delver
Suit and Hero. The Delver suit comes with a Suit Condition
Sheet that you will utilize for your entire campaign. During
the first game you only have one hero, but as you progress
through the campaign you will earn other Heroes to choose
from quest to quest. Whenever you begin a new quest you
can choose 1 hero among the available heroes you have
unlocked throughout the campaign to start a quest with.

Starting Equipment
Like Hero Cards, your suit condition sheet can also provide
you with additional equipment cards to start the quest
with. However when you are playing your first game, these
will not be present and are instead earned throughout
the campaign as permanent upgrades that you can
choose from. When game effects instruct you to mark an
equipment type on your Suit Condition Sheet, you can do
so in any column of your choosing. When you start a quest,
you will only be able to choose 1 column to gain equipment
from. Combining a chosen column on your sheet with
the Hero you choose at the beginning of a quest provides
interesting strategies to start a quest with.

Equipment Upgrades
Your suit condition sheet also tracks equipment unlocks.

Double Damage Tokens
Double Damage Tokens can be spent during a player’s turn
to double the damage of an attack they are performing.
Double Damage tokens come in 4 different types, Light,
Medium, Heavy, and Universal. No matter the type of double
damage token you use, you can only use 1 double damage

Light Double Damage Token: Doubles the
damage output of a Light Damage attack.
Medium Double Damage Token: Doubles the
damage output of a Medium Damage attack.
Heavy Double Damage Token: Doubles the
damage output of a Heavy Damage attack.
Universal Double Damage Token: Doubles the
damage output of any type of damage attack.
Consumable Token Types
AP Tokens
AP Tokens are the most important type of
consumable tokens. They offer a player versatile
ways to utilize their Delver. Spending a specific
amount of AP can allow a player to do the following during
their turn.
1 AP: Spending 1 AP token allows a player to
double their movement value during their turn. (for
example: if a delver has a movement value of 3, if
they spend 1 AP token they can now move up to 6
spaces instead of just 3).
2 AP: Spending 2 AP allows a player to refresh 1
Weapon Card (see Refreshing a Weapon Card on
page xx of the rulebook for more info.)
5 AP: Spending 5 AP allows a player to refresh all
Weapon Cards (see Refreshing Weapon Cards on
page xx of the rulebook for more info.)
AP can also be spent by equipment effects and other
abilities to boost their impact. Managing your AP tokens
well can often be the reason you survive a wave, so make
sure to use them wisely.
Reroll Token
Throughout the game players will roll dice to
determine the results of abilities during combat.
Players may spend a Reroll Token to reroll a
die anytime after it has been rolled before its effects are
resolved. A player may spend a Reroll token at anytime,
even if it isn’t their turn, and even if the player was not the
person that rolled the die.

Taking a turn
Now that you have an understanding of what tools a Delver
can use, let’s explain how to use them to take your turn.
During the Hero Phase, players take alternating turns. Each
turn consists of a Movement step and an Action step, which
may be resolved in either order. Turns continue alternating
until both players have Prepared to Run (see Preparing
to Run on pg. XX), meaning each player will typically take
multiple turns during a single Hero Phase.
Movement
Movement allows Delvers to reposition around the Tide
board to enter attack range and avoid counterattacks. A
Delver may move a number of spaces up to the value shown
on its Delver Board. Movement may be made orthogonally
or diagonally, but a Delver may never move through or end
its movement on a space occupied by an Enemy.
Action
Actions allow players to activate the ability of a Weapon
card or activate the ability of a Weapon slot.
On each turn, a player must take an Action by playing a
Weapon card from their hand into an open Weapon slot on
their Delver Board. After playing the card, they may choose
one of the following:
�Activate the ability on the played Weapon card
�Activate the ability of the chosen Weapon slot
If a player cannot play a Weapon card from their hand
during their turn, they must Prepare to Run instead (see
Preparing to Run, pg. XX).

Using a Weapon Card Ability
The primary way to deal damage to enemies is by using a
Weapon card’s ability. Each Weapon card ability includes
the following elements:
A
Damage: This shows the type of damage dealt when
the weapon is used. The amount of damage each
Damage Type deals can be found on the player’s
Delver Board (see Damage Values, pg. XX).
B
Range: Each Weapon card has a range that shows
which spaces are hit when that weapon is used. This
is most often represented by a pattern of triangles.
The empty space at the center of the pattern shows
the space the attack originates from, which is
the space occupied by the attacking Delver. Each
triangle adjacent to the origin represents a space hit
by the attack. If multiple triangles appear in a row
or diagonal line, count outward from the origin that
many spaces; each of those spaces is hit. A weapon
always deals its damage to every space it hits.
C
Additional Effects: Some Weapon cards have
additional effects that occur either before or after
damage is dealt. Effects that occur before damage
is dealt appear above the weapon’s range. Effects
that occur after damage is dealt appear below the
weapon’s range.
When using a Weapon Card ability, first determine the
attack’s strength and range. Then apply its damage to all
affected enemy spaces, resolving any additional effects
before or after damage is dealt, as indicated on the card.
